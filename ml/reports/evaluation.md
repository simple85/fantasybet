# Integrity detector — evaluation on synthetic data

Generated by `python -m integrity evaluate` over 5 simulated platforms (seeds 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).
Each platform: 12,483 bets from 337 users — 300 recreational, 15 skilled, 7 ring, 6 past_poster, 5 glitch_sniper, 4 insider.
Values are mean ± standard deviation across the platforms.

|                       | luck only   | rules only   | anomaly only   | combined    |
|:----------------------|:------------|:-------------|:---------------|:------------|
| average precision     | 0.34 ± 0.05 | 0.92 ± 0.02  | 0.93 ± 0.03    | 0.93 ± 0.03 |
| precision@k           | 0.40 ± 0.08 | 0.83 ± 0.02  | 0.86 ± 0.03    | 0.86 ± 0.03 |
| flag precision        | 0.40 ± 0.11 | 1.00 ± 0.00  | 1.00 ± 0.00    | 0.74 ± 0.03 |
| flag recall           | 0.20 ± 0.05 | 0.82 ± 0.00  | 0.50 ± 0.00    | 0.93 ± 0.07 |
| skilled flagged       | 5.2         | 0.0          | 0.0            | 2.6         |
| recall: glitch_sniper | 0.32 ± 0.16 | 1.00 ± 0.00  | 0.68 ± 0.24    | 1.00 ± 0.00 |
| recall: insider       | 0.15 ± 0.12 | 0.00 ± 0.00  | 0.00 ± 0.00    | 0.60 ± 0.37 |

Caveat: the cheaters are simulated, so these numbers show the detector finds the patterns it
was designed for. Real abuse will include patterns nobody simulated; that is what the
unsupervised anomaly score is for, and why flags go to a human, not to an automatic ban.
